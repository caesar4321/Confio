# Design: "Tu primer dólar": the assistant becomes Home for people who have never funded

## Problem Statement

a16z's "Where Consumer AI Has Room to Grow" lists Personal finance (PayPal, Mint,
Robinhood) with no AI product in the consumer AI Top 100. A ChatGPT take proposed
that Confío make AI the interface: intent, then AI-composed financial actions, then
the user approves and signs.

Confío's own data says the binding problem comes before that: **almost nobody puts a
first dollar in.** An AI that manages money is worth little while money never arrives.
The opportunity is to make the AI the interface at the moment that decides everything:
the never-funded user, who "doesn't see why".

## Demand Evidence

Measured on prod 2026-10-06. The 15 mule-attack accounts (more than 10 lifetime
rejected top-ups each, up to 175) are excluded from every funnel figure.

| Segment (phone country) | Users | Ever started a top-up | Completed one |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| v1 countries: AR 1,650 · CO 959 · MX 653 · PE 619 · BO 592 · CL 240 | ~4,700 | ~138 | ~54 |
| Brazil (rail exists, no evidence yet) | 98 | 3 | 0 |
| Venezuela (no top-up rail) | 2,026 | 40 | 0 |
| EC / US / ES / PY (no local rail) | ~1,240 | 21 | 1 |
| No phone country (unfinished onboarding) | 2,916 | 0 | 0 |

Completion depends on the era:

- **Guardarian era (Mar–Jun):** orders mostly expired unpaid, so few starters finished.
- **Current Koywe era (Jul–Oct):** 37 of 54 starters completed (69%).
  - By month: Jul 7/11 · Aug 18/21 · Sep 9/18 · Oct 3/4.

So on today's rails the bigger leak is **starting**, about 10–20 people a month, not
finishing. Finishing still loses about 3 in 10, which this design also addresses with
the post-step states.

Other signals:

- **First fundings:** about 1.75 per week over the last 12 weeks. Weekly counts: 1, 1,
  2, 1, 2, 4, 3, 1, 2, 0, 1, 3.
- **The live assistant** (since 2026-10-04: 31 turns, 8 users, $0.016 total cost)
  hears "cómo funciona la app", "no sé nada de app", "cómo pongo mi dinero desde
  Bolivia", "cómo solicito un crédito" and "cargué desde Binance y no llega".
  Navigation is its most-used tool (13 of 21 tool calls).
- **Human support** (418 threads, Mar–Oct): loans (~120 threads) and "what is Confío /
  how do I use it" (~55) are the top two themes. Confío offers no loans.

## Status Quo

Users in v1 countries installed, mostly from Julian's audience, tap around the two
verbs and leave without funding. Asked why, the founder's diagnosis is **"they don't
see why"**: they already have a bank, Binance or Yape, and no reason to hold dollars in
Confío yet. Nothing in the app answers that question in their own terms.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

- **Who:** a person in a personal-account context with phone country AR, BO, CL, CO,
  MX or PE who has never funded (defined below). About 4,650 people.
- **Reach, honestly:** only **15 of them opened the app in the last 7 days and 109 in
  the last 30**, by `last_login`, which may undercount app opens. The hero reaches
  whoever shows up; it does not bring people back.
- **Wedge:** while that person has never funded, Home leads with the assistant asking
  about their situation, not their intent. One concrete, sourced reason follows, then
  one pre-filled step that ends on the real verb screen, which they confirm and sign.

## Constraints

- **The AI never moves money.** Every money step ends on the existing verb screen with
  its own guards, the exact cost shown, and the user's confirmation and signature.
  Proposal-only, per the 2026-10-05 security audit.
- **No TikTok links and no video tutorials** (founder decision): the assistant itself
  guides the person, inside the app.
- **Numbers shown to the user come from code**, never from the model: sourced, dated
  tables, fee configuration or the live in-app quote. This is enforced at runtime (see
  Guardrails), not only by an eval.
- **Rails:** no new Algorand work; BSC rails only. Venezuela is out of scope (rail
  problem, Pago Móvil in progress). Brazil is out of v1 (98 users, 0 completions).
- **Language:** code identifiers in English, user copy in Spanish.
- **No control group** (standing founder decision for the assistant): measure against
  the pre-launch baseline.

## Premises

1. **The binding constraint is the first dollar.** Success = first-funded users in v1
   countries (definition below).
2. **An AI interface does not create need.** The assistant's job is to map the person's
   stated situation to one concrete Confío job (shelter from devaluation, sending to
   family, saving in dollars, "I already have USDT") and the exact first step.
3. **The AI stays read-only and proposal-only.** The person confirms and signs.
4. **Venezuela and no-rail countries are a rail problem**, outside this metric.
5. **5″ (founder, revised after the second opinion):** the AI becomes the primary
   interface, starting with the zero-balance first-dollar moment.
   - It is situation-first ("¿Qué te preocupa de tu plata?" with answer chips), not an
     empty intent box.
   - The full intent-first Home for everyone comes later, and only if this moves first
     funding.
   - **This partially reverses** the 2026-08-01 decision to keep Home's buttons and the
     2026-09 two-verb Home, for eligible never-funded users only. Everyone else keeps
     the two-verb Home.

## Cross-Model Perspective

Independent cold read by a Claude subagent (Codex was out of credits):

- **Steelman:** "The AI is not the product. It turns a person's situation into a first
  funded wallet."
- **Most revealing answer:** "they don't see why". A demand problem, not a navigation
  problem: an intent box asks the one question these users can't answer.
- **Premise challenged:** 5′ (intent-first Home for everyone). At 10–20 top-up starters
  a month a Home rewrite can't be read for months and puts the whole base at risk.
  Prediction: under 20% would type anything into an intent box.
- **New signal:** the first dollar may be USDT already on an exchange ("cargué desde
  Binance y no llega"). The funded definition counts deposits.
- **Proposed prototype:** a 3-question flow, buttons only, one static sourced number
  per answer, one pre-filled step, an honest answer to "crédito". The founder adopted
  the shape but dropped the TikTok entry: the assistant guides the person in-app.

## Approaches Considered

- **A. Proactive bubble:** the bubble opens once for never-funded users. Rejected:
  dismissable popup, and it doesn't make the AI the interface.
- **B. Situation-first Home for never-funded users (chosen).**
- **C. Binance deposit coach:** kept as a branch of B (the "Ya tengo dólares" chip), not
  a standalone flow.

## Recommended Approach: B

### Definitions (server-side, shared by eligibility, the exit rule and the metric)

- **First funded.** The user has either:
  - a `RampTransaction(direction='on_ramp', status='COMPLETED')`, or
  - an external USDT deposit on BSC credited to their Confío balance (the existing
    USDT → cUSD/cUSD+ conversion record) of at least US$1.

  Internal sends from other users, rewards, invite or bonus credits, and legacy
  Algorand-era balances do **not** count.
- **Abuse exclusion.** Excluded if the user is banned, or
  `ramps.koywe.on_ramp_rejection_locked(user)` is true, or they have more than 10
  lifetime rejected on-ramp orders. The 15-account cut used above is the same
  predicate. The metric denominator uses it too.
- **Eligible.** All of the following:
  - the global switch `ASSISTANT_FIRST_DOLLAR_ENABLED` is on;
  - the active context is the user's **personal** account (being an employee elsewhere
    doesn't disqualify; switching to a business account shows that account's normal
    Home);
  - phone country is in {AR, BO, CL, CO, MX, PE};
  - the user is not first funded and not abuse-excluded.

  Any error evaluating this means **not eligible** (normal Home).
- **Global switch.** `ASSISTANT_FIRST_DOLLAR_ENABLED`, a Django setting read from
  `.env.mainnet`, default `False`. Toggled by committing the env change and restarting
  daphne, the same flow as other prod flags.

### What the eligible person sees

1. **Home hero = the assistant, inline.**
   - **Layout:** the hero takes the place of the balance hero and the "Tu mes" month
     strip, which have nothing to show at zero. It uses the same brand tokens as the
     approved Tu mes Home (hero field #047857→#065F46, Instrument Sans). Their pet, a
     greeting with their first name, and the question. Enviar and Recibir stay visible
     below, smaller. After first funding, the Tu mes Home returns unchanged.
   - **Chips**, each shown only when its card can be built:
     - "Mi moneda pierde valor": only where the devaluation rule below shows a loss;
     - "Le mando dinero a mi familia";
     - "Quiero ahorrar en dólares": its card offers Confío Dollar+ only when
       `cusd_plus.eligibility.ONDO_POLICY.evaluate(user, request).allowed`, the same
       predicate the stock and savings screens use, and otherwise Confío Dollar;
     - "Ya tengo dólares (Binance u otra billetera)".
   - **Text field:** one optional field that sends into the existing assistant thread.
     **No mic in v1.** Loans are not a chip: the existing FAQ already answers "¿me
     prestan?" honestly in free text.
2. **One reason, as a card built by code from a tool result:**
   - **Devaluation:**
     - the 12-month change of the **official** local currency rate against USD, from
       one named public source per country, with the as-of date on the card;
     - Argentina also states that the parallel rate differs, without picking the
       scarier one;
     - if the official rate didn't lose at least 5% (for example the pegged boliviano),
       that chip is hidden for the country.
   - **Family:** what it costs to send through Confío: 0% between users; the all-in
     cost to enter and to leave from the fee configuration and the live quote endpoint,
     effective rate against a reference mid rate, spread included. Plus the rails the
     family member can receive on.
   - **Savings:** Confío Dollar+ with the in-app variable rate and "no garantizado", or
     Confío Dollar.
   - **USDT:** the exact network (BNB Smart Chain, BEP-20) and token (USDT), the user's
     own deposit address, a note that incoming USDT becomes their Confío dollar
     automatically (with the entry fee), and the two mistakes that apply on BSC:
     sending on another network (TRC-20 or ERC-20) or another token. **Recovery
     stance:** funds sent on the wrong network generally can't be recovered by Confío.
     The card says so before they send.
3. **One step, as a card that opens the real screen.** The route and params come only
   from the server tool result, never from model text, and every param is allowlisted
   and clamped server-side.
   - Recibir → Recargar with the country's method pre-selected, and a pre-filled amount
     if Open Question 2 allows.
   - The deposit screen.
   - **Live rail check:** the step uses the same live availability the Recargar screen
     uses (`ramps.koywe.on_ramp_paused(country)`, `get_country_ramp_config`, the
     user's rejection lock). If no top-up rail is usable for this user right now, only
     the USDT branch is offered. If that is unavailable too, the user is not eligible
     and sees the normal Home.
4. **After the step** (the hero tracks the latest order):
   - **Awaiting payment** (order created, not paid): shows the order and how to pay.
   - **Expired, rejected, failed or cancelled:** back to the chips, with one plain
     sentence mapped from the order status (never raw provider text) and "Intentar de
     nuevo".
   - **Paid, provider processing:** "Estamos acreditando tu recarga".
   - **Stuck:** paid and processing for more than 24h, or a USDT deposit the user says
     they sent that isn't credited after 1h. This hands off to the human team through
     the existing `escalate_to_human` path (support thread, Susy's queue). The hero then
     shows "El equipo de Confío está revisando tu recarga" until a staff reply.
   - **Completed:** "¡Listo, ya tenés tus primeros US$X!" once; from the next visit,
     Home reverts.

### Guardrails (runtime, not only eval)

- Card payloads (`fact_card`, `step_card`) are attached to the reply by server code from
  the tool result. The client renders only those.
- While the first-dollar tool is active in a turn, the model's text reply passes a
  **number check**: any percentage, currency amount or rate that is not in the turn's
  tool outputs means the text is replaced by a fixed line pointing to the card.
- **Copy** is localized per country: currency name, and neutral-Spanish "tú" by default
  (voseo only for AR). Julian signs off the strings.
- **Older clients** don't declare `firstDollarCards`. They never get cards: they keep
  the normal Home, and any reply they receive stands alone as text.
- The first-dollar eligibility flag is served in its **own query**, so an older server
  can't fail the shared Home query (isolation rule).

### Events (for the metric)

`first_dollar.shown`, `.chip`, `.card`, `.step_opened`, `.top_up_started`, `.funded`.
Each is tied to the user and timestamped, with no message text.

## Open Questions

1. **Devaluation source table:** which public source per country (central bank or
   official reference) and who refreshes it monthly. Our own rate history starts
   2026-10-04.
2. **Pre-filled amount:** does Recibir → Recargar accept a pre-selected method and
   amount via navigation params today, or does `navigate` need an allowlisted numeric
   param?
3. **USDT deposit:** confirm that the `USDCDeposit` route (DepositScreen) shows the BSC
   USDT address and network, and that the deposit → conversion record is queryable for
   the funded definition.
4. **Reach:** with about 15 eligible people a week, should never-funded users get one
   push notification that points to the hero? That is not a video or a link-out;
   founder decision.

## Success Criteria

- **Decision metric (one):** weekly count of newly first-funded eligible users in v1
  countries.
  - **Baseline:** about 1.75 a week over the 12 weeks before launch, top-ups only.
    Deposits are added to both baseline and launch once Open Question 3 makes them
    queryable.
- **Read at 4 weeks** after the store build reaches users.
  - Expected under baseline: about 7. A real doubling (about 14) is detectable at this
    volume (Poisson tail ~2%); smaller lifts are not. If unclear, extend to 8 weeks
    rather than declare success.
  - **Working:** at least 14 first fundings in 4 weeks.
  - **Kill or rethink:** at most 7, or chip engagement under 20% of people shown.
- **Secondary (diagnostic only):** shown → chip → step_opened → funded within 72h.
- **Known confounders, recorded with dates:** the store build (also ships the assistant
  fixes), any Koywe rail changes, the Pago Móvil launch (excluded country, but it may
  shift attention).
- **Guardrails:**
  - no model-generated number shown (runtime check and eval case);
  - top-up completion rate for starters not below the Koywe-era 69%;
  - "no llega" support threads do not rise.

## Distribution Plan

- **App:** the next store build (iOS + Android), which also carries the pending assistant
  fixes. The hero is off for everyone until `ASSISTANT_FIRST_DOLLAR_ENABLED` is turned
  on; there is no app-side switch.
- **Server:** deploys through the existing `ssh confio-prod` flow.

## Dependencies

- Answers to Open Questions 1–3 before engineering review.
- The store build carrying the client changes.
- Pago Móvil (Venezuela) is separate and not blocking.

## The Assignment

Before anything is built, get the exact words of five never-funded people in v1
countries about why they haven't put money in: two from Argentina and one each from
Peru, Bolivia and Mexico.

- **Selection:** the most recently active never-funded users in those countries (from
  the 109 active in the last 30 days), whether or not they ever wrote to support.
- **How:** Susy messages them through the support thread. No videos or links.
- **Fallback:** if a country has fewer than one such user, take the next-most-recent
  from another v1 country.
- **Bias note:** people who answer support messages are more engaged than the silent
  majority. Treat their words as an upper bound on interest.

Paste their words into this doc and rewrite the four chips in their language. If none of
the five fits the chips, the chips are wrong, and that's the cheapest moment to learn it.

### R2-1 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The USDT branch is still built on unconfirmed facts. Open Question 3 hasn't confirmed that the deposit screen shows the BSC USDT address or that the deposit-to-conversion record can be queried. Even so, the doc commits to: the 'First funded' definition counting that record; the USDT card saying incoming USDT 'becomes their Confío dollar automatically'; and the live rail check falling back to 'only the USDT branch'. If the record can't be queried, a user who deposited USDT stays eligible and keeps seeing the hero after funding. The route is also named `USDCDeposit` while the branch is about USDT, and the doc doesn't say whether that screen handles USDT.

**Remedy**

> Before engineering review, confirm in code the BSC USDT detection, auto-conversion and crediting path, the route that shows it, and that the record can be queried. State what v1 does if any of this fails: drop the USDT chip, the deposit clause of 'First funded' and the USDT-only fallback, so eligibility fails closed to the normal Home.

### R2-2 — consistency

**Problem**

> The formal 'Eligible' definition (switch, personal context, phone country, not funded, not abuse-excluded) leaves out rail availability. Step 3 says that if neither a top-up rail nor the USDT branch is usable, 'the user is not eligible and sees the normal Home'. The definitions section claims to be the single source shared by eligibility, the exit rule and the metric, so the two can't both be the rule. Chip visibility under a USDT-only fallback is also unstated: the devaluation, family and savings cards all lead to a top-up step that wouldn't exist.

**Remedy**

> Add live rail/USDT availability to the 'Eligible' definition, and use it in the metric denominator too. Specify which chips appear when only the USDT branch is usable, and what step the devaluation, family and savings cards offer in that case.

### R2-3 — feasibility

**Problem**

> The live rail check names only Koywe predicates (`ramps.koywe.on_ramp_paused(country)` and the Koywe rejection lock), and the Demand Evidence reasons only from the Koywe era. The doc doesn't say which provider serves top-ups in each of the six v1 countries today, or whether any are currently paused or limited per recipient (for example, Colombia after the 2026-09 fraud case). **Remedy**
